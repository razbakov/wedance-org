## 1. Honest evaluation of Kirill's 10-post seed

Read it as someone scrolling at speed, not as the founder who wrote it.

### What's strong

- **Hook is sharp.** "Your Instagram doesn't show your talent" is a real pain that lands instantly with social dancers, performers, and instructors. It's a clean mirror, not a generic "level up your brand" pitch. This is the spine — protect it.
- **Authority is native.** Coming from SDTV ("we see thousands of dancers, here's what we notice") is the only authority play that doesn't feel like a coach selling courses. Lean into it more.
- **Comment-keyword model is right.** ARTIST / PROFILE / GUIDE / BOOKED / SPRINT — IG-native, ManyChat-friendly, captures intent without forcing a website jump. This works.
- **Format mix is balanced.** Reels (top of funnel) + carousels (education) + stories (signal) + offer (conversion) + before/after (proof) — no single format overload.
- **Profile Check as soft conversion is clever.** It gives more value than a PDF, qualifies the lead, and gives Kirill a reason to follow up with a personalized recommendation. Better than a lead magnet.

### What's weak

- **Posts 1–5 say almost the same thing.** Every one of them is a variation of "your profile doesn't show your level." Five posts saying the same thing in a row is one post posted five times. Audience burns out before post 6 (the offer) lands.
- **Post 4 ("If an organizer opens your IG today, what do they see?") and Post 1 are the same hook in different clothes.** Pick the strongest, drop the other.
- **Post 6 is too early for the offer.** In a 10-post seed, the offer post should land at position 7 or 8, after enough proof and education has been seeded. Posting an offer on post 6 reads as "they're already selling."
- **Post 7 (Before/After with real photos) is the most important post in the entire seed and it's buried at position 7.** This should be position 2 or 3. Visual proof is what makes the rest of the captions believable.
- **Post 8 (Founder crossover, "best artists are also personal brands") feels like a different brand voice.** It pulls toward Brand Bureau premium register, not SDTV editorial register. Either rewrite in SDTV voice or move it to a Brand Bureau channel — don't post it on SDTV main.
- **Post 9 (Mini-guide, 7 things) is essentially the same content as the lead-magnet PDF.** Either keep the post and skip the PDF, or keep the PDF and skip the post — running both dilutes the offer for the PDF.
- **No artist-feature post.** Nothing that tags or features a real dancer. SDTV's biggest organic strength is community resonance — posts featuring real, named dancers will outperform any educational post by a lot. Add at least one.
- **No story-only flow described.** Stories drive 60-70% of DM intent on accounts the size of SDTV. The seed is feed-only with one story poll. Underbuilt.

### Predicted engagement (directional, no fabricated numbers)

- Posts 1, 4 will perform well on save and share — clean pain mirrors do.
- Post 7 (before/after with real visuals) will be the highest reach + highest DM driver if executed well.
- Posts 2, 5 will perform medium — useful but not novel.
- Post 6 (offer) will underperform on reach, overperform on DM intent. That's fine — its job is conversion, not virality.
- Posts 8, 9 will be the weakest.

1. **Reel — "Your dancing is strong. But does your profile show it?"** (original 1) — opens the conversation

### NEW post to add (position 4)

**Featured dancer post.** Pick one real, named, willing dancer from SDTV's network. Publish their "before/after" with their permission, on-camera quote, full credit. This is the post that converts the editorial vertical from "SDTV is teaching us" into "SDTV works with real artists." ## 2. Narrative for Russian-period model photographs

This is the most sensitive question in the brief. I'll be direct.

### Honest framing

You can't claim business outcomes from old portrait shoots when you don't have the data, weren't tracking it, and aren't in current contact with the model. That's not "creative storytelling" — that's fabricating case studies, and one screenshot from a dancer who recognizes the photo would end the credibility of the entire site.

But you also don't have to throw the photos away. There's a clean path.

### Recommended format: "Image Studies," not "Case Studies"

Rename the section. `data/cases.ts` becomes `data/imageStudies.ts`. Section title goes from "Selected work" or "Cases" to **"Image Studies"** (or in EN: "Studio Studies" / "From the studio").

The framing changes from "here's what we did for this client and the result" to **"here's how directed image work changes how someone reads on camera."** That's the truth — you actually did the shoot, you actually directed the image, the visual change is real. You just don't claim a business outcome you can't verify.

This is a real, established format in editorial photography (think Vogue's "Before & After" or Avedon's working sketches). It's not a workaround — it's a more sophisticated frame.

### What you can honestly say

- "Studio direction. Light, posture, wardrobe, framing."
- "How the camera reads the same person before and after directed work."
- "An image study from the studio archive."
- "Working notes from a portrait session."

### What you cannot say

- "+3× revenue" (you don't know)
- "0 → 100K followers" (you don't know)
- "Festival invites" (you don't know)
- Anything implying a quantified business outcome you didn't measure

### Anonymization vs named

**Default to anonymized for the Russian-period archive.** Reasons:

1. You don't have current consent to publish their name attached to a "case study."
2. Russia in 2026 is a politically charged context — naming someone Russian-based without their fresh sign-off can cause them harm you don't see from Barcelona.
3. Anonymized + "Image Study #023" reads more editorial, more premium, more Brand Bureau register. Names read more "client showcase."

**Named is reserved for fresh BCN work where the model has signed a release and explicitly agreed to be featured.** That's the goal state — start there as soon as you have one BCN shoot done.

### Disclaimer / transparency line

Add a single line on the Image Studies index page, in small editorial type:

> *Image Studies are working selections from the studio archive. Some sessions are recent client work; some are studio studies and exercises in direction. We name people only with their explicit permission.*

This protects you legally and ethically, signals editorial seriousness, and gives you full freedom to use the archive without misrepresenting it. No one reads this and downgrades the brand — if anything, the disclaimer reads more premium.

### Legal — flag for Marco/Viktor

I'm not the legal owner of this, but the questions you'll need answered before publishing:

1. Did you have a model release signed at the time of each Russian-period shoot? If yes, what scope (commercial use, web use, indefinite)?
2. For shoots without a release, can the photo be used non-identifiably (cropped, back-of-head, silhouette, blur)?
3. GDPR exposure — if any subject is now EU-resident, "right to erasure" applies. Build a takedown protocol.
4. Russia/Belarus exposure — for any subject still based in those territories, named publication on a EU-hosted site can create issues for them, not for you. Default anonymous.

→ Marco / Viktor own the legal and process side. Luna recommends: **anonymize by default, named only with fresh signed release on file.**

## 3. Three case-narrative templates (EN, ready to drop into `data/imageStudies.ts`)

All three follow the same pattern: **who → before → intervention → after → 3 image study notes.** No fabricated business outcomes. Visual change is the deliverable. Transformation language stays inside what a portrait session can credibly claim.

### Template A — Performer / dancer (closest to SDTV)

```text
n:    023
who:  N. — Performer, contemporary
cat:  performer
title: Stage presence, on camera

before:
"Strong on stage, unreadable on a still frame. Direct camera contact, frontal posture,
neutral wardrobe — the same energy that worked under stage light disappeared in studio
light. The image read as 'a dancer at a casting,' not 'a performer with a body of work.'"

intervention:
"Three-quarter framing. Wardrobe shifted to a single restrained palette so the body
reads as the subject. Direction moved away from posed stillness toward held mid-movement.
We worked the eyes — looking past the camera, not into it."

after:
"The same person reads as a working artist instead of an audition headshot. The portrait
holds the room the way the performance holds the stage."

notes:
- Light direction: side-key, soft fill, no fashion sheen
- Frame: 4x5 vertical, shoulders cropped at the third
- Wardrobe: matte black, single texture, no logos
```

### Template B — Founder / expert (sample for Brand Bureau core positioning)

```text
n:    014
who:  T. — Founder, B2B technology
cat:  founder
title: From product photo to public presence

before:
"Most founder photography lives in two modes — the LinkedIn headshot or the product
demo. T. arrived with both, and neither showed the person an investor or client would
actually be sitting across from. Posture suggested defensiveness. Wardrobe was correct
but invisible. The image was professional and forgettable."

intervention:
"We rebuilt around a single editorial portrait: seated, three-quarter, hands relaxed
and visible. Wardrobe simplified to indigo and graphite, no patterns, no statement
pieces. Light from a single window-source, no studio rig, so the face reads as a
person, not a product."

after:
"The portrait now reads as the founder of a serious company, not the photographer's
attempt at making one. Same person, fully visible."

notes:
- Hands in frame — credibility signal for founders
- Single environmental cue (chair, table, window) — no styled set
- Restrained color — lets the face carry the frame
```

### Template C — Creative / artist (broader, non-dance)

```text
n:    029
who:  K. — Visual artist
cat:  performer
title: From self-portrait to authored image

before:
"K. had been photographing herself for years. Strong eye, but the work read as
self-documentation — a record of being there, not a presentation of the work. The
portraits looked like the artist saw herself, not how a curator or gallery would
encounter her."

intervention:
"We took the camera out of her hands. New register: full direction, working in series
rather than single frames. Three sessions, each built around one editorial idea
(hands, studio, the work itself). Wardrobe sourced from her own closet — nothing
purchased, nothing borrowed."

after:
"A coherent visual identity that holds across portfolio, press, and her own channels.
The artist became a person you can introduce, not just a body of work you have to
explain."

notes:
- Series over singles — three frames > one perfect frame
- Studio in frame, controlled — context as authority
- Wardrobe-from-life — credibility signal for creatives
```

These are written in Brand Bureau register: editorial, restrained, no superlatives, no business-outcome claims, no fake metrics. Drop them in directly.

## 5. Three reel-script drafts (15-30s, fresh — not edits of Kirill's 10)

### Reel 1 — "The Receipts Test" (20s, hook + 3 beats + CTA)

```text
HOOK (0–3s):
On-camera, direct: "Open your Instagram. Now imagine a festival booker just opened it."

BEAT 1 (3–8s):
Cut to phone screen. Scrolling a real (anonymized) dancer profile.
Voice-over: "The first 9 squares are the audition."

BEAT 2 (8–14s):
Hold on grid. Highlight: bio with no offer, mixed content, no pinned reel.
Voice-over: "Bio doesn't say what's bookable. The pinned reel is from a class.
Their best moment is buried in the feed."

BEAT 3 (14–18s):
Cut back to camera. "It's not your dancing. It's the receipts."

CTA (18–20s):
"Comment ARTIST. We'll send you the 4-point profile review."
```

**Why this one:** "Receipts" reframes the issue from "your image" (which feels like criticism) to "your evidence" (which feels like advocacy). Same diagnosis, different psychological response. Lower defensive bounce.

### Reel 2 — "What an Organizer Actually Sees" (25s, POV format)

```text
HOOK (0–3s):
Phone screen, finger scrolling. Top text: "POV: organizer is choosing between you
and three other artists."

BEAT 1 (3–10s):
Scroll through a generic-looking profile. Tap bio — no clarity. Tap reels — class
clips. Scroll grid — random selfies and party photos.
Top text updates: "5 seconds in. They're not seeing what you do."

BEAT 2 (10–17s):
Switch profiles. Now scrolling a directed profile — bio crystal clear, pinned reel
performance, first 9 grid squares all aligned visually.
Top text: "5 seconds in. They know if you're booked."

BEAT 3 (17–22s):
Side-by-side freeze. Same level dancer, two profiles.
Top text: "Same talent. Different decision."

"DM us 'BOOKED'. We'll show you what the second profile is doing differently."
```

**Why this one:** No talking head, fully visual, works without sound — strongest format for SDTV's algorithmic reach. POV format is the highest-ceiling reel structure in 2026 for educational verticals.

## 6. SDTV editorial vertical — naming + visual separation

### Name evaluation

**"Artist Image Check by SDTV"** — Kirill's working name. Honest read:

- Pro: descriptive, says what it is.
- Con: "Check" is a weak, transactional word. It frames the vertical as a service ("come get checked"), not as an editorial publication ("come read this"). Limits the vertical's authority over time. Also, "check" reads slightly cheap in EN — like "credit check" or "background check," not like editorial.

### Better name options (ranked)

1. **"SDTV Studio Notes"** — editorial, evergreen, doesn't tie to the offer, leaves room for the vertical to grow. Reads like a Vogue column. **Recommended.**
2. **"Artist Image — by SDTV"** — keeps the keyword "Artist Image" (good for offer continuity), drops the weak "Check"
3. **"On Camera"** — short, premium, dance-adjacent, ownable as a hashtag (#OnCameraSDTV). Strong if SDTV wants to own a column-name as IP.
4. **"The Profile Desk"** — playful editorial (like a magazine "desk"), works for carousels and lighter-tone reels
5. "Artist Image Check" (Kirill's original) — keep only if we want offer-obvious naming for SEO/discovery; otherwise demote

**Recommended pick: "SDTV Studio Notes"** as the umbrella name for the editorial vertical, with "Artist Image" as the offer name (pricing page, application, ManyChat). Vertical gives editorial cover; offer stays sharp.

### Visual separation from main SDTV feed

This matters more than the name. Without visual separation, this content dilutes the main SDTV feed into "SDTV that sometimes teaches things," which weakens both.

**Recommended visual signature for the vertical:**

1. **One consistent corner badge.** Small, bottom-left, on every post in the vertical: "SDTV Studio Notes" in a single typeface, 60% opacity. Audiences learn it within 3-4 posts.
2. **Color grade shift.** Main SDTV = saturated, warm, party register. Vertical = desaturated 15-20%, slight cool shift, more editorial. Keeps the same brand world but signals "different room."
3. **Typography lockup.** Main SDTV uses dynamic, energetic type (caps, bold). Vertical uses a single restrained serif or thin sans for all overlay text. Instantly readable as "different content type."
4. **First-frame composition rule.** Vertical posts always open with negative space — never start on a face or action. Trains the eye that this is "the slower content."
5. **No heavy emoji, no party hashtags.** Main SDTV uses both freely. The vertical does not.
6. **Carousels in the vertical use one image style only** — desaturated, single-color background, editorial type. Recognizable in the explore feed within 0.5s.

The goal: someone scrolling past 10 SDTV posts can identify a Studio Notes post in less than a second, without reading the caption. That's how you protect both the main feed (party energy intact) and the vertical (editorial seriousness intact).

### Cadence

Kirill's original "every 6-7 SDTV posts" is right. Don't lower it (vertical drowns in main feed); don't raise it (main feed loses its core identity). One vertical post per 6-7 main posts. If main feed is 4 posts/week, vertical is 1-2 posts every 2-3 weeks. That's enough to build the audience without choking the host channel.

## 7. Voice contradiction — SDTV vs Brand Bureau

This is the real strategic risk. Worth handling carefully.

### The contradiction (real, not theoretical)

- **SDTV voice:** loud, communal, party energy, dance floor, FOMO, "you have to be there," exclamation marks, emojis, present tense, motion. Music is loud. The brand is *with* the dancers.
- **Brand Bureau voice:** quiet, restrained, editorial, premium, single sentences, no exclamation, no emoji, considered tense, stillness. The brand is *above* — a studio, a directorial gaze, a craft.

If you bolt Brand Bureau directly onto SDTV, two things break:

1. SDTV's audience smells "selling" the moment the register goes premium. Drop in engagement on the main feed.
2. Brand Bureau loses its premium signal because it now looks like an SDTV upsell, not an independent studio.

You can't run both voices in the same channel without one cannibalizing the other.

### The bridge — three layers

The solution isn't to merge the voices. It's to **build a third voice in the middle that sits between them, lives only in the editorial vertical, and acts as the bridge.** Three layers, three voices.

**Layer 1 — SDTV main feed (unchanged):**
- Loud, communal, party, FOMO. Stays exactly as it is. Don't touch.

**Layer 2 — SDTV Studio Notes (the bridge voice — NEW):**
- Calmer than SDTV. Warmer than Brand Bureau.
- Still talks like a peer to dancers ("we see this all the time," "you know the feeling when…"), but with editorial restraint.
- Drops the emojis but keeps the warmth.
- First-person plural ("we") — the studio voice, not the founder voice.
- Sentences are shorter. Periods, not exclamations.
- Premium without being cold.
- Example: "Strong dancer. Quiet image. This week's profile review."
- Example: "We watched 50 dancer profiles this month. Same pattern in 40 of them. Carousel below."

**Layer 3 — Brand Bureau (unchanged premium register):**
- Quiet. Editorial. Single-frame thinking. The studio.
- This voice lives only on brandbureau.vercel.app and on (if/when created) a separate Brand Bureau IG.

### Migration path through the funnel

A dancer enters at Layer 1 (SDTV main, party energy). They see one Studio Notes post (Layer 2 — the bridge voice — peer + editorial). They DM ARTIST. ManyChat reply uses Layer 2 voice ("hey, glad you reached out — let's take a look"). The Profile Check is delivered in Layer 2 voice (warm, editorial). If they convert to Artist Image Starter, the email sequence and onboarding shift gradually toward Layer 3 (the studio voice). By the time they're in the studio doing the shoot, they've been onboarded to Brand Bureau register.

The audience never feels a register-jump because each layer overlaps the next by about 30%. They graduate.

### What to avoid

- Don't post Brand Bureau (Layer 3) copy on SDTV main (Layer 1). That's where 90% of voice failure happens.
- Don't post SDTV (Layer 1) copy on the Brand Bureau site. That's the other 10%.
- Don't try to write a "unified brand voice." There isn't one. There are three voices that share a worldview.
