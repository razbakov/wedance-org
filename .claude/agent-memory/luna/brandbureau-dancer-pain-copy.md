## §1 — Pain language map (3 segments, in their voice)

The instruction was first-person, the way they actually think. Russian where it lands hardest, English where the audience defaults to it. No editorial polish — these should feel uncomfortable to read because they ARE uncomfortable to think.

### Segment A — Social Dancer (TAM ~400k, identity pain)

WTP: €99-500. The vast majority. Most never become teachers. Their pain is not commercial — it's social/existential. They want to belong, be seen, be recognized at the festivals they already pay to attend.

```
1.  "Я хорошо танцую. Меня никто не зовёт показать соло на пати."

2.  "Все знают [имя]. Я уже четыре года танцую — меня не замечают."

3.  "Прихожу на фестиваль — никто не подходит первым.
     Будто меня там нет."

4.  "Я снимаю клипы, постю — лайки от своих же.
     Незнакомые не подписываются."

5.  "Девочки/мальчики уровня ниже меня уже 'кто-то' в комьюнити.
     Я — просто ещё один на джеме."

6.  "I dance every weekend for years.
     Nobody outside my city knows my name."

7.  "Хочу, чтобы когда я приезжаю на конгресс — меня узнавали.
     Не звезда. Просто 'а, это та, которая…'"

8.  "Мне неловко представляться 'я танцую'. Звучит как хобби.
     А я хочу, чтобы это звучало как кто-то."
```

The pattern: **invisibility, not unbookability.** They are not trying to monetize. They are trying to exist as a recognized human in their tribe. This is much closer to how Lululemon sells than how a coaching course sells.

### Segment B — Aspiring Teacher / DJ / Performer (~75k, hybrid pain — SWEET SPOT)

WTP: €500-3k. Mix of identity AND functional. They want recognition AND bookings. The current Profile Check copy already serves this segment well. Pain language here should COMPLEMENT, not replace, the bookability frame.

```
1.  "Я даю классы в своём городе — за пределами никто не знает.
     Хочу, чтобы звали как минимум на конгрессы региона."

2.  "У меня есть стиль. Но мой Instagram — как у любого
     социального танцора с телефоном."

3.  "Я не хуже [имя на международной сцене].
     Просто меня никто не упаковал."

4.  "Постю год — приглашений нет. Пишу организаторам — игнор.
     Чувствую, что что-то делаю не так, но не понимаю что."

5.  "Хочу перейти из 'хорошего танцора' в 'артиста'.
     Не понимаю, где эта грань — в навыке или в подаче."

6.  "Я устал(а) быть 'местным феноменом'.
     Хочу, чтобы организатор сам написал, а не я ему."

7.  "У меня есть бренд в голове. На профиле — каша."
```

The pattern: **transition pain.** They are between identities. The Profile Check already converts here. The new vertical adds emotional weight underneath the functional pitch.

### Segment C — Established Artist (~25k, identity reinforcement + commercial)

WTP: €1.5-5k+. Already known in their lane. Pain is more subtle — about staying relevant, levelling up, maintaining premium perception. They will never admit this pain publicly. So the copy must offer them dignity, not surface it.

```
1.  "У меня есть имя в [Cuban / NY / Bachata / Kizomba] —
     но за пределами никто не знает. Я в своём пузыре."

2.  "Меня зовут на одни и те же фестивали уже три года.
     Хочу попасть в другой эшелон. Не понимаю, что не так
     с моей подачей."

3.  "I'm booked. But I'm booked at the same fee I was three
     years ago. My image is still the 2022 me."

4.  "У молодых артистов сейчас визуал сильнее моего.
     Я держусь на репутации — но это ненадолго."

5.  "Я уже не хочу 'profile check'. Я хочу образ как у
     [международное имя]. С таким же ощущением серьёзности."

6.  "Хочу, чтобы когда мой пресс-кит открывают —
     это читалось как уровень, а не как папка с фото."

7.  "I built a career. The image around it has not caught up."
```

The pattern: **dignity, not diagnosis.** This segment buys the Signature tier or doesn't buy at all. They don't want to be told what's wrong. They want to be invited into a higher room.

### Cross-segment observation

Segments A and B both contain the word "не замечают" / "никто не знает". Same root pain (invisibility), different commercial expression. This means **one piece of identity-language hero copy can pull both segments**, then route them inside the funnel by ManyChat to different price points.

## §2 — Hero copy: 3 variants for sub-brand landing

Goal: name the identity pain in 4 seconds. Premium register. Russian-first because that's where the SDTV audience is densest, English subline for portability.

### Variant 1 — "Танцуешь давно. Тебя ещё не знают." (RECOMMENDED)

```
HEADLINE:
Танцуешь давно. Тебя ещё не знают.

SUBLINE:
Talent isn't recognition. We work with dancers
whose level the room already feels — and turn it
into an image the rest of the scene can finally see.

PRIMARY CTA:
Получить Identity Check  →

BELOW-FOLD — what you'll get (identity language, NOT feature):

·  An image that arrives before you do at the next festival.
·  A profile that makes someone scroll, stop, and ask:
   "кто это — почему я не знаю".
·  Тебя, наконец, представленного(ую) так, как ты
   ощущаешь себя на паркете.
·  An invitation into a room where being a working dancer
   is taken seriously.
```

**Why this wins:** "Танцуешь давно. Тебя ещё не знают." is the cleanest mirror of Segment A pain in Russian. It doesn't say "your profile is bad" (defensive). It says "you've been here a long time, and that's not your fault — but it's also not okay." The room itself is the problem, not the dancer. That's a much more sophisticated psychological move than the current "your IG doesn't show your level" angle.

The subline does the lifting that the headline can't — it names "the room already feels you," which is the existential validation Segment A is starving for. Then it commits to a deliverable ("an image the rest of the scene can finally see").

The bullets are deliberately vague-on-features and specific-on-feeling. "An invitation into a room where being a working dancer is taken seriously" is a Brand Bureau-register sentence, not a coaching sentence. It signals premium without listing photos/videos/bio rewrites.

### Variant 2 — "You dance like somebody. Your scene treats you like nobody."

```
HEADLINE:
You dance like somebody.
Your scene treats you like nobody.

SUBLINE:
For dancers who've put in the years and still feel
invisible at the festivals they already attend.
We build the image that closes that gap.

PRIMARY CTA:
Apply for an Identity Check  →

BELOW-FOLD:

·  Walk into the next festival as a name, not a number.
```

**Why it's strong but not the winner:** English-first removes 60% of the SDTV native audience cognitively. Also "nobody" is a hard word — it converts the Segment A demographic well but it's high-risk. One screenshot of "treats you like nobody" pulled out of context = bad press inside the dance community ("SDTV is calling our community toxic"). The ROI is real but the downside is unmanaged.

Use for Meta retargeting where the audience is already qualified, not as the primary site hero.

### Variant 3 — "Уровень есть. Имени нет."

```
HEADLINE:
Уровень есть. Имени нет.

SUBLINE:
Premium image practice for dancers who are tired
of being good and unknown at the same time.

PRIMARY CTA:
Apply  →

BELOW-FOLD:

·  Image direction that treats your years on the floor
   as the foundation, not the pitch.
·  Photos and video that show you the way the floor
   already sees you.
·  Положение в комьюнити, которое раньше брали только
   те, у кого был "правильный" учитель.
·  A studio file you can hand to an organizer without
   apologising for what's in it.
```

**Why it's the contrarian pick:** "Уровень есть. Имени нет." is the shortest possible expression of the entire Segment A pain. Four words, no metaphor, premium register, native to the language the audience uses. The downside is it's the most depressive — it states the problem flatly with no warmth. That works for Segment C (Established Artists who don't want a coaching tone) but underperforms for Segment A (who need warmth in addition to truth).

If we ever ship a Brand-Bureau-only version for the Established tier, this is the headline. For the SDTV-side mass funnel, Variant 1 wins.

### Defended pick: VARIANT 1

Variant 1 is the right launch headline because it:

1. names the pain without insulting the dancer
2. makes the SCENE the problem (recognition mechanic), not the dancer (image mechanic)
3. holds the warmth that makes Segment A buy (which is most of the volume)
4. still works as a soft headline for Segments B and C with no rewrite
5. avoids the words "Instagram", "profile", and "bookable" — which immediately collapses the copy back into the existing functional voice

Test Variant 2 in retargeting against qualified audiences only. Hold Variant 3 for an eventual Established-tier landing page.

## §3 — Naming: 5 sub-brand candidates

Test criteria: emotional resonance · premium signal · doesn't muddy Brand Bureau master · doesn't sound like a fitness program · pronounceable in EN/ES/RU.

### Candidates

**1. SDTV Atelier** — *RECOMMENDED*

Score: emotional 8/10 · premium 9/10 · BB-clarity 9/10 · cringe-risk 1/10 · pronounceability 9/10

"Atelier" is a working word in the European fashion/photography world (Lumen Atelier, Maison Margiela, Atelier de Beauté), reads instantly as premium without being precious. "SDTV" carries the trust capital of a 1M+ follower brand. "Atelier" creates the third room — neither party (SDTV main) nor strategy boardroom (Brand Bureau master) but a working studio.

Critically: "Atelier" reads as a **place where image work is done**, which gives the entire vertical a physical-feeling identity. Dancers can imagine walking into the atelier. They cannot imagine walking into "Artist Track."

Risk: minor brand-confusion with Lumen Atelier (Kirill's broader image practice in Memory). But: per the latest sprint-input file (§15), Lumen Atelier IS Brand Bureau — they've converged. So calling the dancer vertical "SDTV Atelier" actually reinforces the master-brand atelier identity rather than fragmenting it. **Use this naming convergence as an asset, not a problem.**

**2. SDTV Artist Track**

Score: emotional 4/10 · premium 5/10 · BB-clarity 7/10 · cringe-risk 4/10 · pronounceability 9/10

The proposed name. It works functionally but it sounds like a fitness program, a Spotify playlist tier, or a corporate L&D module. "Track" is a transactional word that signals progression, not identity. For Segment B (Aspiring Teachers, transition pain) it's adequate. For Segment A (Social Dancers, identity pain) it actively repels — you don't want to be on someone's "track," you want to be somebody. Reject.

**3. SDTV Atelier — The Image Studio**

Score: emotional 8/10 · premium 9/10 · BB-clarity 8/10 · cringe-risk 1/10 · pronounceability 8/10

Same as #1 but with a tagline-locked sub-name. Useful if "SDTV Atelier" alone reads too sparse on early posts. Drop the sub-tag once the brand is established. Don't lock the sub-tag into URLs or logo lockups — keep it as flexible editorial typography.

**4. SDTV Studio**

Score: emotional 6/10 · premium 7/10 · BB-clarity 5/10 · cringe-risk 2/10 · pronounceability 9/10

Clean and short. But "studio" is an overcrowded word — every photographer, every coach, every YouTube creator runs "a studio." It doesn't differentiate from competitors. And it conflicts with Studio Notes (the editorial vertical from your prior content strategy) — using the same root word for both the editorial surface and the sub-brand creates ambiguity.

**5. SDTV Identity**

Score: emotional 9/10 · premium 5/10 · BB-clarity 6/10 · cringe-risk 7/10 · pronounceability 9/10

Names the pain directly. But "Identity" as a brand word is now heavily associated with HR/diversity/political registers, and in Spanish/Russian markets it reads as imported corporate. Also: "SDTV Identity Check" (the product name that follows) reads like a security service. High emotional pull, but operationally fragile. Reject.

### Defended winner: SDTV Atelier

Use the convergence with Lumen Atelier (per Memory §15) as a feature not a bug. The vertical naming aligns with the master-brand naming, the master-brand benefits from SDTV reach, and the dancer audience gets a name that signals premium without using corporate vocabulary.

Product names underneath:
- **SDTV Identity Check** (free, replaces "Profile Check" for this vertical)
- **SDTV Atelier — Profile Fix** (€150-250)
- **SDTV Atelier — Image Starter** (€500-900)
- **SDTV Atelier — Signature Presence** (€1.5-3.5k+)
- **SDTV Atelier — Visibility Retainer** (€600-1.5k/mo)

Note: keep "Profile Check" alive as the existing-vertical name for Aspiring Teacher functional copy. Don't rename retroactively. The Atelier ladder runs in parallel.

## §5 — Content bucket mix (post-launch ongoing)

After the 5-week launch seed, the ongoing rhythm needs a defended ratio. Opinionated split:

```
35%   Identity reflection      — pain mirrors, scene observation,
                                  no offer, no diagnosis
20%   Before / After proof     — image studies, with permission
20%   Community spotlight      — featured dancers, named, no sales
15%   Educational              — how the image works, light pedagogy
10%   Soft offer               — Identity Check, ladder mention
```

### Why these numbers

**35% Identity reflection** is high on purpose. The Identity vertical is fundamentally a desire-engine, and desire is built through repeated mirror-and-name, not through education. If the educational content rises above 20%, the vertical drifts toward coaching register and burns the audience. The single most important content type here is "we see you" content with no pitch attached. Most coaching brands underweight this and over-rely on educational — that's why they read as cringe.

**20% Before/After** because proof is the only thing that converts identity-pain in this niche. Less than 20% and the entire vertical reads as theory. More than 20% and it reads as a portfolio brag. Hold to ~1 in 5.

**20% Community spotlight** because SDTV's organic strength is community resonance. A featured dancer post (named, with their voice in the caption) outperforms anything else on engagement. Use this consistently — but the rule is: spotlight is NOT a transformation post. It's recognition of someone who's already there. Mixing them collapses both formats.

**15% Educational** is deliberately low for this vertical (compared to ~35% on the Aspiring Teacher track). Identity-pain audience does NOT respond to "5 things to fix in your bio" — it reads as "you are doing it wrong, here's homework." Same audience DOES respond to "we noticed this pattern in 38 profiles this week," which is editorial observation, not education. Reframe.

**10% Soft offer** is the conversion floor. Below 10% and the funnel doesn't move. Above 10% and the audience burns out. The 10% should ALL come through identity language, never feature language ("get an Identity Check" not "book a strategy call").

### What is explicitly NOT on this list

- No motivational content. No "you are amazing" posts. No transformation declarations. No "I used to be invisible too" founder origin reels. These are the cringe failure modes — see §7.

## §6 — Voice guide (8 sentences)

The new SDTV Atelier vertical has a distinct voice that sits in the middle of the existing 3-layer architecture (per `brandbureau-content-strategy.md` §7). Here is how it sounds:

1. **Quieter than SDTV main.** No exclamations. No emojis. No "let's go" energy. The vertical never tries to match the party. It's the room where the noise stops.

2. **Warmer than Brand Bureau master.** The Atelier voice acknowledges feeling — it can say "we know this feels invisible" — where the master brand would never name an emotion that directly. Master brand is editorial; Atelier is editorial-with-a-pulse.

3. **First-person plural ("we"), never first-person singular ("I"). The Atelier is a studio. Studios speak as a collective. This also keeps Kirill's individual voice protected for the Founder-voice posts (Post 8, rare) where the singular "I" lands harder because it's used sparingly.

4. **Russian and English mixed, naturally.** Most of SDTV's audience is bilingual or multilingual. Russian for the emotional anchor sentences ("Уровень есть. Имени нет."). English for the structural sentences ("We work with dancers whose level the room already feels"). NEVER translate one to the other in the same post — let each language do what it does best. Spanish kept for ES site only.

5. **Names the pain in the dancer's own words, not in coaching vocabulary.** Bad: "you may be experiencing impostor syndrome around your image." Good: "уровень есть, имени нет."

6. **Talks about the SCENE as the unit of analysis, not the dancer.** "The scene doesn't see you yet" beats "your profile is weak." This shifts the locus of the problem from the dancer (defensive) to the structure (curious). Same diagnosis, different psychological response.

7. **Uses words from the world we want to be in, not the world the audience is leaving.** Atelier vocabulary: studio, frame, direction, image, room, name, scene, presence. NOT: profile, bio, content, posting strategy, growth hack, algorithm, engagement, conversion. The second list is the fitness-coach lexicon. The first is the editorial-photography lexicon. The Atelier lives in the second world.

8. **Specificity over superlatives.** "An image that arrives before you do at the next festival" beats "transform your online presence." Brand Bureau master rule applies here: every sentence should be either specific or restrained — never both vague and grand.

### Side-by-side examples (same idea, three voices)

```
Idea: "We help dancers look more recognized."

SDTV main voice:
   "🔥 We're opening an image studio for dancers!
   You dance hard, your profile should look like it.
   Comment ATELIER 👇"

SDTV Atelier voice:
   "Танцуешь давно. Тебя ещё не знают.
   We started a studio for that gap.
   Comment ATELIER for your Identity Check."

Brand Bureau master voice:
   "Image direction for working dancers.
   Studio days available in Barcelona, May–July.
   By application."
```

The Atelier voice is the bridge. It uses Russian like SDTV main but drops the heat. It uses studio language like Brand Bureau master but drops the coldness. It speaks to feeling like a peer but with editorial restraint. **This middle voice is the entire reason the funnel works.** Lose it, and the bridge collapses into either party-noise (SDTV) or premium-distance (BB master), and the dancer audience falls through.

## §7 — Five copy traps that kill this vertical

These are the failure modes that make identity-language content read as generic transformation / fitness-influencer / IG-coach cringe. Each one happens when a writer forgets that this audience is a tribe with taste, not a coaching market.

### Trap 1 — Transformation language

**Cringe:** "Transform your online presence in 30 days." / "From invisible to fully booked." / "Step into your power as a dancer."

**Why it kills:** "Transform" is the single most overused word in coaching marketing. The dance audience hears it and instantly mentally categorizes the brand alongside fitness-influencer products and online-business courses — both of which are seen as low-status in the social-dance scene. "From X to Y" framing is also fine in B2B but reads as cheap in cultural niches with strong taste.

**Fix:** Use "rebuild" or "direct" or "re-frame" or "show" or simply "start." "We rebuild the image around what's already there" beats "transform your image."

### Trap 2 — Direct mirror of the audience's deepest insecurity

**Cringe:** "You feel invisible. You feel forgotten. You feel less-than."

**Why it kills:** Yes, the pain map (§1) names exactly these feelings. But putting them in marketing copy verbatim — especially at the beginning, before trust is built — reads as manipulative. The audience knows what they feel. They don't need a brand to tell them. What they need is a brand to NAME the structural reality without forcing them to admit the emotion publicly.

**Fix:** Let the audience self-recognize. "Танцуешь давно. Тебя ещё не знают." names the situation without naming the emotion — the dancer fills in the feeling themselves. That's much stronger than naming the feeling for them. **Trust the audience to do half the work.**

### Trap 3 — "Founder origin story" as proof

**Cringe:** "I used to be the invisible dancer at every festival. Then I rebuilt my image. Now organizers fly me out. Here's how."

**Why it kills:** This is the entire IG-coach genre in one paragraph. The dance scene has seen 200 of these in the last 3 years and has fully developed antibodies. Posting one will not just fail to convert — it will tank the brand's perceived seriousness. Also: Kirill's actual founder story isn't this. He's the videographer/founder behind SDTV, not a dancer who rebuilt his career. Forcing this trope on an audience that knows him would read as fake.

**Fix:** Founder voice (Post 8) speaks AS the studio operator, not AS a transformed-dancer-now-coaching-others. "За 10 лет в индустрии я снимал тысячи танцоров" is a credible founder line. "I used to be invisible too" is not. Stay in the operator/observer position.

### Trap 4 — Numerical promise language

**Cringe:** "Get 10x more bookings." / "Double your festival invitations." / "Reach 50K dancers in your niche."

**Why it kills:** Two problems. First, none of these are true — image work doesn't reliably 10x bookings, and the brand cannot defend the claim. Second, the entire numerical-promise register is owned by online-business-coaching marketing, which is the LOWEST-status category in the dance scene's mental hierarchy. Even when the numbers are accurate, the format itself signals cheap.

**Fix:** Replace numbers with named outcomes. "An image that arrives before you do at the next festival" is much stronger than "get 3x more festival invitations" — and it's also defensible. The Atelier is in the image business, not the booking-multiplier business. Don't borrow promises from the coaching genre.

### Trap 5 — Scarcity theatrics

**Cringe:** "Only 4 spots left this month!" / "Closing applications in 24 hours!" / "Last chance for the early-bird tier!"

**Why it kills:** This audience is sophisticated about marketing. They've seen scarcity theatrics on every IG-coach landing page since 2019. The Atelier is positioned as a premium working studio — premium studios don't run countdown timers. Real scarcity is fine ("we take 4 image clients per month, May is open") because it's information. Manufactured scarcity ("ENDS TONIGHT") is the death signal of the coaching genre.

**Fix:** State capacity as a fact, not a hook. "Atelier opens 8 studio days in May. Applications are reviewed weekly." That's the right register. It signals premium because it doesn't try to.

### One sentence that captures the avoidance discipline

> If the line could appear unedited on a "before-and-after weight-loss coach" Instagram, it does not belong on the Atelier feed.

This is the test to apply to every caption draft.

## §8 — Comparison: replace or run in parallel?

The strategic question. Here's the call.

### The two voices

**Voice A — Functional / Bookability (existing).** Maps to Aspiring Teacher segment (~75k). Pain language: "your profile doesn't show your level," "your IG isn't bookable," "the first 9 grid is the audition." Product ladder: Profile Check → Profile Fix → Image Starter → Signature → Retainer.

**Voice B — Identity / Recognition (proposed in this file).** Maps to Social Dancer segment (~400k) and reinforces Aspiring Teacher emotionally. Pain language: "тебя ещё не знают," "уровень есть, имени нет," "the scene doesn't see you yet." Product ladder: Identity Check → Atelier Profile Fix → Atelier Image Starter → Atelier Signature → Atelier Visibility Retainer.

### Recommendation: PARALLEL, not REPLACE

The instinct to replace would be wrong. Three reasons:

**1. The functional voice is already converting on Segment B revenue.** Aspiring Teachers (€500-3k WTP) is the actual sweet spot. They are the ones who pay for Image Starter and Signature. The functional copy is what gets them in the door and keeps them feeling competent (they can self-diagnose "yes my IG isn't bookable, I see the gap"). Replacing this voice with identity-language risks making the highest-paying segment feel weird ("are you saying I'm invisible? I just wanted my reels to look better").

**2. The identity voice unlocks the larger TAM at LOWER price points.** Social Dancers (~400k, WTP €99-500) are the volume play, not the margin play. The Identity voice converts this segment into the lower tiers (€150 Profile Fix, €500 Image Starter), and a percentage of them graduate upward. The two voices feed two different price points and two different volumes.

**3. Voice substitution at the brand level usually fails.** Brand Bureau / Lumen has been broadening its register since v2 (per Memory). Replacing the artist functional voice with identity voice would cause the SAME register collapse — the brand starts speaking only to one segment, loses the other. Better to run two surfaces (or two ManyChat flows, or two landing pages) under the same Atelier umbrella, each speaking to its segment in its language.

### How parallel works structurally

```
Single funnel umbrella: SDTV Atelier
                              │
                ┌─────────────┴─────────────┐
                │                           │
        IDENTITY entry                  ARTIST entry
        (Segment A pain)                (Segment B/C pain)
                │                           │
        Identity Check (free)         Profile Check (free)
        ManyChat: ATELIER             ManyChat: ARTIST
        Voice: identity-warm          Voice: functional-clear
                │                           │
                └─────────────┬─────────────┘
                              │
                Same product ladder underneath:
                Profile Fix · Image Starter ·
                Signature · Visibility Retainer
```

Same products. Two entry doors. Two voices. ManyChat tags route to two different first-message sequences. Both ladders converge on the same delivery (the studio actually delivers the same work).

### Content cadence under parallel

- **SDTV main feed** — keeps the existing artist/functional content for Segment B (~30% of vertical content). Adds Atelier identity content for Segment A (~70%). The split reflects the TAM ratio.
- **Studio Notes editorial vertical** — runs identity content primarily, with occasional functional crossovers.
- **Brand Bureau site** — both ladders live on /artists, with two clear path entries: "If you want your image to match what the floor sees" (identity) vs. "If you want a profile that's clearly bookable" (functional).

### One-line summary

> The Aspiring Teacher functional voice is where the money is. The Social Dancer identity voice is where the audience is. Run both under SDTV Atelier. Don't pick — let the dancer self-select which mirror they want to look in.
