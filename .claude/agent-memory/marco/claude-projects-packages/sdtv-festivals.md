# SDTV Commercial Model — Value Flip, KPIs, Client Transition

**Status:** active strategic reference (2026-04-26)
**Owners:** Кирилл (Growth/Sales), Wife (Delivery/Margin), Marco (advise), Kai (advise)

---

## Mantra

**Production is not the product. Production is the fuel. Promo is the product.**

Camera → replaceable. Editor → replaceable. Photographer → replaceable.
**Channel with accumulated community trust, attention distribution, scene context, and existing dance audience → NOT replaceable.**

The strategic mistake of the past: devaluing the scarcest asset (channel access to community attention).

---

## Why the channel is the moat

### How festivals think about videographers
- Festivals **rotate videographers by design** — they want different camera styles each year
- Videographer = replaceable commodity in their eyes
- **Even perfect delivery doesn't guarantee renewal**
- Decision is made on the festival's plan, not on your performance
- **Real case observed:** content not yet delivered → next year already given to another videographer

### What festivals actually need (but don't always articulate)
- Visibility, ticket sales, community engagement, brand authority
- They NEED promo — but historically got it bundled cheap or free with production
- They're used to paying for cameras, not for reach

### Community dynamics (B2C side that powers B2B)
- Offline events = heartbeat of the social dance world
- Community is tight-knit, reputation-driven
- Dancers follow content creators (SDTV channel) not just events
- **SDTV channel IS a community touchpoint** — not just a service provider's portfolio

---

## Old vs new model

### Old model (still active with current clients)
```
PRODUCTION (core) → promo as addon/discount → sell packages
```
- Clients hired SDTV as videographers first
- Promo bundled, often underpriced or free
- Clients perceive value as "filming" not "reach"
- Easy to replace, no switching cost, no moat
- Revenue depends on physical presence at events

### New model (target for new clients)
```
PROMO & DISTRIBUTION (core) → production as supporting layer
```
- Old positioning: "We film festivals and add promo."
- **New positioning:** "We help festivals stay visible, desirable, and talked about — before, during, and after the event. Production is one layer of that system."

---

## Three products

### 1. Promo / Distribution Product
Sold WITHOUT on-site presence.
- Collab campaign · Countdown campaign · Artist spotlight package · Post-event visibility cycle · Next-edition hype package

### 2. Event Media Product
On-site physical delivery.
- Video coverage, photo, stories, recap · On-site capture, live content

### 3. Visibility Partner Product
Annual or seasonal package — hardest to replace with "another camera."
- Pre-event promotion + live event coverage + post-event distribution + next-edition launch

---

## Client transition rule (CRITICAL — agent behavior)

| Client | Default model | Agent behavior |

| **Renewal** | Build case for promo value with data (views, reach, engagement) | Use impact recap as the bridge to new model |

## North Star: Promo-Led Revenue

Revenue from visibility / promo / distribution products (not just filming).

**Includes:** promo packages, collab campaigns, artist/festival spotlight, pre-event hype, post-event visibility cycle, annual/seasonal visibility deals.
**Excludes:** pure filming, pure photo, travel reimbursements, technical one-offs without promo value.

**Target 2026:** +50% to +80% vs promo revenue 2025.

1. **Promo Revenue Share** — Target Q4 2026: 40-55%. <30% production-heavy; 30-40% transition; 40%+ model is changing.
2. **Remote Revenue Ratio** — Target 2026: 25-35%. Less dependent on travel calendar.
3. **Client Renewal Rate** — Target 2026: 55-70%. Only count clients with real renewal window.
4. **Average 12-Month Client Value** — Target 2026: +25% to +40% vs 2025 base. Account value per year not earnings per trip.

# SDTV Negotiation Playbook — Scope Protection + Decision-Maker Alignment

**Status:** active baseline rule (2026-04-26)
**Owners:** Кирилл (final), Marco (advise/prepare), Kai (advise)
**Applies to:** all festival/organizer deals — pre-agreement, post-agreement, renewals.

---

## Founder principle

We value the relationship, but we do not allow vague scope, moving conditions, or silent expansion of work. **Our word matters, and the client's word matters too.**

---

## Core rule

When a festival team member questions price, days, or deliverables **after the agreement was already discussed with the organizer** — do not argue, over-explain, or defend the price. Realign with the decision-maker.

---

## How to apply (8 rules)

1. **Confirmed agreement with the main organizer = source of truth.** Not whatever a new team member now assumes.
2. **New team member reinterprets scope?** Politely pause and realign with the decision-maker. Do not negotiate sideways.
3. **Never expand scope for free** just to avoid tension.
4. **If SDTV accepts extra coverage**, exchange it for stronger value:
   - 2-year commitment · clearer scope · better logistics · improved commercial terms (price, payment timing, exclusivity)
5. **After every phone call or verbal handshake** — send a short written recap confirming: year · dates · exact nights/days covered · type of coverage · what is excluded · conditions agreed
6. **Tone:** calm, respectful. **Substance:** firm.
7. **Avoid "full coverage"** — never say it unless paid and explicitly confirmed in writing.
8. **Use "social dance video coverage"** instead of "videos". SDTV sells media coverage, positioning, and festival visibility — not a fixed number of clips.

---

## Escalation rule

Agents must escalate to **Кирилл (Founder)** before replying when post-agreement questions touch:
- pricing · scope (deliverables, video count, photo count) · travel · hotel · number of days / nights · workshops coverage · shows coverage · social media obligations

**Why escalate:** these all carry margin and brand-positioning risk.

---

## Recap template (use after every call)

Hi [Name],

Quick recap to keep us aligned:

- Festival: [Name] [Year]
- Dates covered: [Day1] – [DayN] (nights: [N1, N2, ...])
- Coverage type: social dance video coverage [+ optional: photo / show / artist promo]
- Excluded: [what's NOT in scope]
- Conditions: [payment terms, deadlines, deliverable timing]

Let me know if anything here doesn't match your understanding.

Best,
Kirill / SDTV

---

## Why this exists

Pattern observed in real deals: organizer agrees to scope and price → team member (logistics, social media, artistic director) joins later → reopens discussion → tries to expand scope or push price down. Without this rule, SDTV silently absorbs cost, dilutes positioning, and trains clients that scope is negotiable post-agreement.

This rule protects: Margin · Positioning · Relationships · Founder bandwidth.
