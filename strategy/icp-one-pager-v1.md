> **Главное правило:** Главный ICP не geography, не size — это **mindset**. Organizer понимает, что медиа влияет на продажи, репутацию и желание людей приехать.

> **Strongest formula for agents:**
> SDTV sells **visibility** to festivals that are already good in real life, but are under-presented online.

---

## 1. Core ICP Definition

SDTV's best client is **not** "a festival that needs video".

Our ideal client is a festival organizer who already has a real event, wants stronger market perception, and understands that visibility before, during, and after the festival directly affects ticket sales, reputation, artist value, and next-year growth.

**SDTV is positioned as:** Festival Media & Positioning Partner

Not:
- freelance videographer
- random content creator
- one-day video crew
- cheap social media manager
- promoter-code affiliate

We help festivals look active, desirable, premium, and culturally relevant online.

---

## 2. Primary ICP — Growth Festival Organizer

**Who they are:**
- Salsa, bachata, mambo, kizomba, zouk, or mixed Latin/social dance festival
- Strongest territories: Spain, Germany, Italy, France, Netherlands, Poland, Czech Republic, Croatia, Turkey, UK, US, international circuits where Kirill has reputation
- 300–2,500 attendees
- At least 2 editions completed
- Real artists, DJs, socials, workshops, shows, community presence
- Organizer is known in the scene or wants to become more visible
- Cares about brand perception, not only logistics
- Knows Kirill personally / knows SDTV / can be reached via trusted mutual connections

**Best version of this client** (the strongest match):
Already has a decent or strong festival, but the online presence does not fully reflect the quality.

They might have:
- good artists but weak promo
- good atmosphere but poor storytelling
- great dance floor but no structured content pipeline
- nice event but inconsistent Instagram
- strong past edition but no momentum between editions
- many videos from dancers, but no controlled brand narrative

This is where SDTV fits perfectly.

---

## 3. What They Really Want

They may say:
- "We need video"
- "Can you make reels?"
- "Can you come and film?"
- "Can you help us promote?"
- "Can you post us on your channel?"

Underneath:
- more ticket sales
- more trust from dancers
- stronger online presence
- more social proof
- better-looking brand
- content that makes the festival feel alive
- artists and attendees reposting
- next edition easier to sell
- to not disappear after the event
- to look bigger than competitors

**Real desire:** "Make our festival look like something people should not miss."

---

## 4. Main Pain Points

**Before festival:**
- Ticket sales slower than expected
- Social media inconsistent
- No clear promo calendar
- Too dependent on artist reposts
- Content does not create urgency
- Competitors look more active online
- Don't know what to post in final 60–90 days

**During festival:**
- Too busy running event
- Nobody captures right moments strategically
- Content random, delayed, low quality
- Great moments not turned into visibility
- Artists/dancers/atmosphere not documented properly

**After festival:**
- Momentum dies quickly
- Some content, then disappear
- No use of content to sell next edition
- No structured archive
- No case-study recap
- No clear perception upgrade after the event

---

## 5. What They Currently Buy

Most festivals already buy:
- videographer
- photographer
- aftermovie
- social reels
- artist promo videos
- random content help
- maybe basic SMM freelancer

They usually do NOT buy:
- structured distribution
- content calendar around ticket phases
- festival positioning
- promo narrative
- SDTV channel exposure
- pre-event + live + post-event content system
- media strategy connected to sales goals

**This is the gap SDTV owns.**

### 2. Distribution
SDTV ecosystem visibility: SDTV Instagram, SDTV YouTube, artist reposts, dancer reposts, festival collaborations, community reach, cross-event credibility.

### 3. Positioning

**Key line:**
**"We do not only capture the festival. We help the market perceive it better."**

## 7. Whale Signals (✅ go)

A festival is likely a strong SDTV client if:
- They think in editions, not one-time transactions
- They want to grow event over several years
- Already invest in artists, venue, lighting, sound, decor, experience
- Care how the event looks online
- Understand media affects sales and reputation
- Audience that reposts and engages
- At least some budget for promo, not only logistics
- Respond fast when value is clear
- Trust Kirill's taste and industry understanding
- Open to packages, not only hourly/day rates
- Ask about results, reach, content use, next edition
- Want to look premium, international, active

**Strongest buying signal:** "They already know their festival is good, but they feel the online image does not show it strongly enough."

---

## 8. Weak Signals (⚠ caution, may still buy with boundaries)

- Ask only "how many videos?"
- Compare SDTV with local videographers
- Want one fixed deliverable with no strategy
- Don't understand why distribution matters
- Only care about lowest price
- Expect full SMM but provide no content/approvals/direction
- Want results but have no ticket strategy
- Expect SDTV to fix a weak event with content alone

Not auto-NO, but require **clear boundaries**.

---

## 9. Anti-ICP (deprioritize / avoid)

### 9.1 Commodity Buyer
Sees SDTV as: "just a videographer", "someone with a camera", "one reel package", "cheap content for IG".
Language: "Can you make discount?", "We only need 1 day", "Our friend can film, but maybe you can do cheaper", "How many videos exactly?"
**Problem:** They buy labor, not positioning.

### 9.2 First-Time Low-Budget Organizer
Risk: no proven audience, no clear budget, no artist lineup, no content plan, no ticket traction, expects SDTV to create credibility from zero, expects partnership without money.
**Exception:** strong capital, strong network, premium concept, strategic long-term potential.

### 9.3 Fully Self-Sufficient Digital Festival
Has strong in-house media team / distribution / SMM / content engine.
**Not "bad client"** — but the value gap is smaller. Can sell:
- special collaboration
- SDTV distribution as external credibility
- documentary angle
- artist-focused content

### 9.4 High-Control, Low-Trust Organizer
Micromanages every shot, doesn't trust creative judgment, slow approvals, scope creep, treats SDTV like staff. **Drains founder energy.**

### 9.5 Low-Reputation or Chaotic Festival
Bad communication, artist payment drama, poor organization, low attendee satisfaction, negative community reputation. Even if they pay, **brand association may hurt SDTV.**

## 14. Qualification Questions (Kai/Marco use before pitching)

### Event Maturity
- How many editions have you already done?
- How many attendees did you have last edition?
- What is your target for next edition?
- What changed this year vs last year?

### Sales and Promo
- When do ticket sales usually slow down?
- What are your strongest sales months?
- What content usually performs best?
- Do you already have a promo calendar?

### Media Gap
- Do you feel your online image reflects the real quality of the festival?
- What did you miss from last year's media coverage?
- Do you already have someone handling reels, stories, posting?
- What do you want people to feel when they see your festival online?

### Budget and Fit
- Do you already have a media budget for this edition?
- Are you looking only for event coverage, or also promotion before and after?
- One-time package or support across the full festival cycle?

**🎯 Best diagnostic question:**
**"Do you feel your festival looks online as strong as it feels in real life?"**

## 15. Simple Fit Score (Maya gate)

Score each festival 1–5 across 6 dimensions:

| Dimension | 1 (low) | 5 (high) |
|-----------|---------|----------|
| **A. Event quality** | weak event | strong real-life experience |
| **B. Online gap** | already perfect online | strong festival, weak digital |
| **C. Budget potential** | no budget | clear budget for media/promo |
| **D. Relationship warmth** | cold unknown | Kirill knows them / strong mutual |
| **E. Growth mindset** | wants cheap video | wants long-term positioning + sales |
| **F. Brand value for SDTV** | low reputation | strong association, good portfolio |

**Total interpretation:**
- **26–30:** Whale priority
- **21–25:** Strong opportunity
- **16–20:** Possible, needs careful package
- **10–15:** Low priority
- **<10:** Avoid unless strategic reason

## 16. North Star ICP Examples (5 archetypes)

### Type A — Premium Growth Festival
Strong artists, good attendance, international audience, wants premium image online.

### Type B — Good Festival With Weak Social Presence

**Angle:** "Your festival deserves a stronger digital image."

Good event, sales need momentum in final months.
**Angle:** "Let's turn content into desire and urgency before the event."

### Type D — Existing Client With Upgrade Potential
Already bought video/coverage before.
**Angle:** "Last time we captured the event. This time we can build the full visibility cycle."

### Type E — Artist-Led / Community-Led Festival With Strong Trust
Organizer has community authority but weak media system.
**Angle:** "You have the trust. We help package it for the market."

## Adjustments from v0 (logged)

What Kirill specifically corrected from Marco's v0:

1. **"Лично знает Кирилл" не должен быть жестким фильтром** — это сильный booster, но high-fit cold festival тоже можно брать, если есть понятная точка входа.
2. **300–1500 слишком узко** → 300–2,500. Маленький festival может быть стратегическим, если он premium / растущий / даёт доступ к сильной сети.
3. **Anti-ICP не должен включать всех, кто строит свой digital ecosystem.** Это клиент с меньшей болью, но ему можно продавать external credibility / SDTV distribution / documentary angle / artist visibility.
4. **Главный ICP — mindset, не geography.** Страна вторична.
5. **Fit score добавлен** (Section 15) — чтобы агенты не спорили абстрактно, а быстро решали.
